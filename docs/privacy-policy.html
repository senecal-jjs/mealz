Mealz Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026

Mealz is a meal-planning app. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it. The short version: Mealz has no accounts, analytics, ads or tracking. The only things that leave your phone are the photos of recipes you scan and the record of your scan credit.

What we collect

Mealz has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The only information it sends anywhere is the photos of recipes you scan and, if you buy scan credit, a record of your credit (see below). Nothing else leaves your phone.

What stays on your phone

To work, the app stores the following only on your device: recipes you've added yourself and their photos, the recipes in your plan, your grocery list and checked items, items you add yourself, your favourites, your cooking history and ratings, and your preferences (diet, allergies, disliked ingredients, household size and units). This information never leaves your device. If your phone is backed up to iCloud, it may be included in that backup under Apple's terms.

Camera and photos

If you add your own recipe, you can photograph a recipe page or choose a photo from your library. Mealz only sees the photos you pick. It first reads the text in the photo on your phone, using Apple's built-in text recognition. It keeps any dish photo you add in the app's own storage, on your phone; dish photos are never uploaded.

Scanning recipes

Recipes you scan are read by Anthropic's Claude, and Mealz tells you so before your first scan. The photos of the recipe's pages and the text your phone reads from them are sent over an encrypted connection to the Mealz scan server, which passes them to Claude to turn them into a recipe. The result comes straight back to your phone. If you'd rather not, you can type recipes in instead; nothing is sent then.

The Mealz server does not store the photos or the recipe and does not log their contents. It keeps a count of scans per day against a random ID created on your phone, to limit misuse; the ID is not linked to you, and counts are deleted after a week. Anthropic processes the photos under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train its models on them; see Anthropic's privacy policy for how long it may retain API data.

Scan credit

After a few free scans a month, reading a scan on the Mealz server uses scan credit, which you buy in the app through Apple. Mealz has no accounts: your credit belongs to a random wallet ID created on your phone and kept in its keychain, shown as your recovery code. If you're signed in to iCloud, the ID is also saved in your own iCloud (Apple's key-value storage), so your credit comes back on a new iPhone; Mealz can't read your iCloud. The Mealz server records each credit purchase (Apple's transaction ID and the amount) and each paid scan against that ID, and keeps these records to show your balance and handle refunds. They are not linked to your name, Apple ID or anything else about you. Payment is handled entirely by Apple; Mealz never sees your payment details. The server's database is backed up to cloud storage (Tigris) so your credit isn't lost.

iCloud backup

If you're signed in to iCloud with iCloud Drive on, recipes you add to Mealz (and their photos) are backed up to a hidden Mealz folder in your own iCloud Drive, so they come back on a new iPhone. Only your devices can read it; nothing goes to the Mealz server. You can remove it in Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage → Mealz.

Notifications

If you start a cooking timer, Mealz asks for permission to send notifications so it can tell you when the timer ends. These notifications are scheduled locally on your phone; no server is involved.

Sharing

When you share your grocery list, Mealz hands the text to the iOS share sheet and you choose where it goes. Mealz does not see or keep what you send.

Deleting your data

Go to Profile → Reset all data, or delete the app. Either removes everything Mealz stored.

Children

Mealz does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
